\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper has introduced BEACON, a digital twin-inspired supervisory-control interface for autonomous drone swarms in Maritime Search and Rescue operations.
By integrating an explainable path-planner, a real-time mission interface, a comprehensive evaluation framework, and an alert system, BEACON aims to enhance operator situational awareness and reduce cognitive load while supervising multiple drones.
Grounded in HCI principles like the Proximity Compatibility Principle and WCAG guidelines, BEACON's design seeks to address the identified gaps in MSAR-specific multi-agent supervision frameworks.

The analysis does not claim that BEACON's design has resolved the issue of workload entirely, rather it argues, based on theory, and simulation, that BEACON's approach provides a coherent and extensible foundation for future empirical research and real-world testing.
In doing so, BEACON seeks to address the Research Questions outlined earlier in this paper, emphasising analysis and design grounded in existing research, while clearly recognising the limits of the current work.
